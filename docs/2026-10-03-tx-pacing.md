# TX pacing: what it does, and what has and has not been shown

## The change

`kiss.Port` now paces writes by estimated air time. Before handing a frame to
the transport it waits until no more than one full-size frame of air time is
outstanding at the TNC, sized from `ota_baudrate`. Gated by `tx_pacing`
(default true).

## Why

A KISS TNC accepts frames as fast as the link will take them and queues them
internally. A full window of I-frames therefore lands in the TNC in
milliseconds and then takes seconds to transmit. tncd's `tx` counter means "we
handed it to the transport", never "it went on the air". Three known failures
live in that gap:

- A radio that buffers internally accepts the whole burst and reports success
  while nothing is radiated. The UV-PRO holds frames ~2 minutes
  ([2026-10-02 report](2026-10-02-uvpro-bluetooth-tx-report.md)).
- L2 can queue a retransmit of a frame the TNC has not started sending.
- Serial write-pattern corruption at 57600 on the TS-2000 is a symptom of
  handing a device more than it can absorb.

The companion is the link-aware T1 floor (206bc01): the floor exceeds the
pacing delay, so L2 cannot retransmit a frame pacing is still holding back.

## What has been shown

**The mechanism works.** Unit tests in `kiss/pacing_test.go` cover the air-time
estimate, the idle-channel case (no added latency), the bounded backlog, and
abort-on-teardown. End to end through `writerLoop`, a five-frame burst at 1200
baud reaches the wire over 1.44s instead of 23µs, matching the predicted air
time.

**It does not break a healthy link.** The 10KB sustained-upload e2e test -- the
one written for the original OTA pacing stall -- passes with pacing on, over a
real 1200-baud audio path against Dire Wolf's AX.25:

| arm | runs | result |
|---|---|---|
| paced | 2 | PASS, 88s, 49 I-frames, max N(R) 49, 0 retransmits |
| unpaced | 2 | 1 PASS identical to above; 1 FAIL at 300s (31 of 46 acked) |

## Hardware result: it does not fix the UV-PRO stall

Measured 2026-10-03, after this document first claimed only "no evidence of
benefit". There is now evidence, and it is negative. A three-run A/B against the
UV-PRO and the real Winlink gateway, with an independent Dire Wolf receiver
witnessing the actual RF:

| run | pacing | frames handed over | reached the air | lost | outcome |
|-----|--------|-------------------:|----------------:|-----:|---------|
| 1 | on  | 37 | 25 | 32% | stalled at 40% of upload, connection lost |
| 2 | off | 25 | 16 | 36% | 2m07s dead air, connection lost |
| 3 | on  | 12 |  1 | 92% | never connected (11 SABMs swallowed) |

Same binary, same radio, same gateway, same monitor; only `tx_pacing` changed.
**Pacing makes no difference to this failure**, and that is informative rather
than merely disappointing: feeding the radio strictly slower than the channel
can drain rules out host-side overrun of any queue as the cause. The radio is
not being handed more than it can take. It stops radiating what it already
holds, and the frames surface a minute or two later, after the session is dead.
The evidence is in
[2026-10-02-uvpro-bluetooth-tx-report.md](2026-10-02-uvpro-bluetooth-tx-report.md).

So pacing does not address the failure it was built for. What remains is the
narrower rationale it still serves:

- L2 can no longer queue a retransmit of a frame the TNC has not begun sending.
- The TS-2000's write-pattern corruption at 57600 is a separate symptom of
  handing a device more than it can absorb, and pacing bounds that directly.

Both are real, and neither is the UV-PRO. Keeping pacing on by default is a
judgement call, not a measured win: it has a passing regression gate and no
observed cost, but its headline motivation did not survive contact with the
radio. `tx_pacing = false` disables it per port.

## What has NOT been shown

**That pacing helps the case it was built for.** The hardware A/B above says it
does not.

This test cannot demonstrate one, and that is worth recording so nobody
re-runs it expecting an answer. 10240 bytes at 1200 baud is ~68s of payload air
time and the transfer takes 88s, so the channel is already near saturation.
There is no headroom for pacing to show up in either direction, and the paced
and unpaced passing runs are indistinguishable: 88s, 49 I-frames, 48 RRs, max
N(R) 49, zero retransmits, in all three.

**The one unpaced failure is not evidence.** It was initially read as pacing
fixing the stall -- 31 of 46 frames acked, dying at 300s, which is exactly the
signature pacing targets. Then the unpaced re-run passed in 88s with 49/49.
A wireplumber restart had happened immediately before the failing run, which is
the likely cause. Audio level was 25 at the receiver in every run, so the path
quality was not what differed.

n=1 per arm would have produced a confident and wrong conclusion here. It took
a second run per arm to see it.

## That measurement has now been run

It is the section above. The A/B was exactly the test this document originally
called for -- same binary, same radio, only `tx_pacing` changed, with an
independent monitor watching the RF instead of trusting tncd's TX counter --
and it returned a negative result. Recording that is the point: the mechanism
argument for pacing was sound and the hardware still said no.
